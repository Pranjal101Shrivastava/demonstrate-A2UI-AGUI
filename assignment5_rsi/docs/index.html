Assignment 5 · Section 10 Research Studio · RSI Masterclass (October 2026 edition)

SignalForge: recursive self-improvement of a traffic-signal controller

All 17 codelabs of Section 10, plus a synthesis lab, implemented progressively on one problem. The thing being improved is a Python program: the controller that decides, second by second, when a four-way intersection switches its lights. Three state-of-the-art RSI methods (Budget RSI, SO-RSI and SwarmRSI) are rebuilt from the course's paper summaries and compared under one fair protocol. Everything runs on a laptop CPU with the Python standard library.

The problem

Environment

A queueing simulator: 4 approaches, 2 phases, 2 s saturation headway, 2 s start-up loss, 4 s clearance. 8 traffic families: light, heavy, N/S- or E/W-dominant, morning ramp, stadium surge, night, platoons.

Benchmark

An instance is solved if average delay ≤ 18 s and no car waits > 70 s. Splits: 16 fit · 48 steering · 100 held-out. Search code physically cannot read held-out (enforced and unit-tested).

Improved object

A genome rendered to real Python source with up to five mechanisms (actuated gap-out, max-green cap, pressure switching, starvation guard, skip-empty) and their constants. The seed fixed-time plan solves 31/100.

Fair comparison

One cost unit = one instance simulation. Every method gets the same total budget. Readout labels and diagnostic probes are charged. Results are reported as mean ± sd over seeds.

Three RSI methods, head to head

Budget RSI arXiv:2610.29121

80.2/100 held-out

A linear readout on 8 diff coordinates scores edits before running them, and HGM/Thompson selection expands only the top child. Recursive variant: the readout refits itself on every expansion (lab 10.05).

SO-RSI arXiv:2610.05701

96.0/100 held-out

Watches its own edit trace for recurrence, opposing edits and expectation mismatch, then runs cost-charged diagnostic probes on the failing instances and keeps causal findings in inquiry memory.

SwarmRSI arXiv:2610.20431

85.2/100 held-out

Five agents with different research directions, feedback-guided mutation, complementary recombination, and a swarm procedure that evolves its own recombination rate and step size.

Baselines at the same 4,800-unit budget: random search 76.4, Naive (first-order) RSI 69.6.

What the evidence supports, and what it does not

  • SO-RSI's gain survives every control we tried. Removing the hand-written mechanism map barely matters, so the gain comes from anomaly-gated sweeps on the failing instances. Probing after every failure (no anomaly gate) loses about 10 instances (lab 10.07).
  • Budget RSI depends on where its labels come from. A readout trained on random-walk parents ranks the strong parents HGM actually expands worse, and loses about 12 held-out instances (lab 10.04). Its gain over the parent-rate ablation is small and noisy, as in the paper.
  • SwarmRSI's result comes from diverse starting hypotheses. Ablating the shared board, recombination or procedure evolution is within noise at this budget (lab 10.11).
  • Negative result: the RSIFlow meta-agent re-routes work but ties a static workflow (lab 10.13). Course demo audit: the reference SO-RSI demo hard-codes its success rates (lab 10.01).
  • These are laptop adaptations. They preserve each paper's mechanism, not its scale or headline numbers.
